14 days on the street

Open each day, complete the lesson, and check the box when you can explain the topic without reading your notes. The suggested daily study time is 90–120 minutes.

01Welcome to Rural DevelopmentAgency roles, program families, and the big picture

Today's mission

  • Explain what USDA Rural Development does and how housing programs differ from farm lending through FSA.
  • Distinguish Single-Family Housing from Multifamily Housing.
  • Learn the difference between a program overview, a handbook, a regulation, and a current funding notice.

Memory hook

Think of USDA Rural Development as a neighborhood with different doors. First identify the door, then read the rules for that door.

Practice task

Explain in 60 seconds who administers Section 502 Direct, who makes a Guaranteed loan, and why FSA is not the same office as Rural Development.

02Section 502 Direct Home LoansBorrower eligibility, income concepts, repayment, packaging

Today's mission

  • Learn the purpose of the program and its basic borrower and property eligibility categories.
  • Distinguish annual income, adjusted annual income, and repayment income. These are related but not interchangeable.
  • Identify the role of the packager and the role of USDA in final decisions.
  • Locate the current HB-1-3550 handbook and packaging resources.

Practice task

Use a fictional household. List what you need to verify before saying whether the household appears eligible. Do not promise approval based on income alone.

03Single-Family Housing Guaranteed LoansPrivate lender role, USDA guarantee, program comparison

Today's mission

  • Explain who originates a Guaranteed loan and what USDA's guarantee means at a high level.
  • Compare Guaranteed with Direct without mixing their income limits or underwriting steps.
  • Learn to check the current Guaranteed program handbook and lender resources.

Memory hook

Direct: USDA lends. Guaranteed: a participating lender lends, and USDA backs the loan under program rules.

Practice task

Make a two-column comparison of the Direct and Guaranteed programs using only current official sources.

04Section 504 and Mutual Self-HelpRepair assistance and technical assistance grants

Today's mission

  • Describe the purpose of Section 504 Home Repair Loans and Grants and identify its separate eligibility requirements.
  • Explain that Mutual Self-Help Housing Technical Assistance Grants support eligible organizations providing technical assistance, not automatic grants to every homebuyer.
  • Find the current official program page and verify eligible uses and applicants.

Practice task

Match each of three fictional needs to the program to research: buying a home, repairing an existing home, or an organization coordinating mutual self-help construction.

05Land purchase options and the 30-day questionProperty selection, timelines, contracts, and documentation

Today's mission

  • Identify what your training material means by “land purchase options 30 days.” Do not assume it is a universal USDA rule without checking the exact context.
  • Review the current handbook, application checklist, and any official written instructions that describe timing, property selection, purchase agreements, or an option period.
  • Learn the difference between a real-estate contract's option period, a program's application timeline, and a USDA document or eligibility deadline.
  • Record the source title, section, revision date, and exact wording in your study notes.
Rule-check checkpoint: A “30-day” phrase can refer to different processes. Before advising an applicant, verify which event starts the clock, what action is required, whether extensions exist, and which program the rule applies to.

Practice task

Create a timeline for a fictional buyer. Mark the date of each event and write a source beside every deadline. If you cannot verify a deadline, mark it “confirm with USDA,” not “rule.”

06Income limits in all 50 statesState, county, household size, program, and fiscal year

Today's mission

  • Learn why there is no single nationwide income number that applies to every RD housing program.
  • Look up limits using the correct program, location, household size, and current effective date.
  • Separate income eligibility limits from the income calculation used to determine repayment or adjusted annual income.
  • Build a reference sheet with all 50 states as rows and columns for Direct, Guaranteed, and Multifamily sources, effective dates, and links. Fill values from official tables, not memory.

Practice task

Pick Texas plus two other states. For each, identify the official source and write down the applicable program, household size, county or area, effective date, and limit. Then repeat for a different household size.

07Single-family eligibility mapsAddress checks and what a map result does not prove

Today's mission

  • Use the USDA eligibility site and choose the relevant Single-Family Housing program.
  • Test sample addresses and record the date and program selected.
  • Understand that area eligibility is only one part of a review. It does not guarantee borrower eligibility, dwelling eligibility, or loan approval.
  • Learn how to escalate ambiguous or borderline results to the appropriate USDA office.

Practice task

Check three fictional or publicly listed addresses in different communities. Save the result and note exactly which program's map you used.

08Multifamily Housing Direct LoansAffordable rental housing, applicants, and projects

Today's mission

  • Explain the purpose of USDA Multifamily Housing Direct Loans.
  • Identify the types of applicants and rural rental housing projects described by the current program materials.
  • Distinguish project financing from rental assistance to tenants.
  • Find current program guidance, application materials, and funding announcements.

Practice task

Write a one-paragraph overview of the program, then list three questions you would need answered before deciding whether a proposed property or applicant fits.

09Multifamily Housing Loan GuaranteesPrivate financing, guarantees, and project eligibility

Today's mission

  • Describe how the guarantee supports eligible private financing for qualifying rural multifamily housing.
  • Identify borrower, lender, project, and agency roles.
  • Compare the guarantee structure with Multifamily Direct Loans without assuming their eligibility rules are identical.

Practice task

Draw a simple flow: applicant/project → lender → USDA guarantee review. Annotate the flow with questions that must be verified in current guidance.

10Multifamily Housing Rental AssistanceTenant assistance versus property financing

Today's mission

  • Explain the purpose of Rental Assistance in participating rural rental properties.
  • Distinguish a tenant-focused subsidy from a loan or grant used to finance a building.
  • Learn where to verify participating-property, tenant, owner, and contract requirements.

Practice task

Explain the difference between financing the building and helping an eligible tenant afford rent, using your own words.

11Housing Preservation GrantsEligible organizations, repair activities, and funding notices

Today's mission

  • Learn the purpose of Housing Preservation Grants and the kinds of preservation or repair work the program supports.
  • Identify eligible applicants and eligible activities in the current notice.
  • Understand that these grants are not automatically direct grants to any individual homeowner.
  • Check whether funding is currently open and what match, reporting, or service-area conditions apply.

Practice task

Review a current or archived official notice and extract applicant eligibility, eligible activities, deadlines, funding limits, and required attachments.

12Housing Preservation & RevitalizationPreserving existing affordable rural multifamily housing

Today's mission

  • Understand the program's preservation and revitalization purpose and the kinds of eligible multifamily housing needs addressed.
  • Learn how restructuring, rehabilitation, and preservation differ from financing a brand-new single-family purchase.
  • Verify current program availability, eligible borrowers, property requirements, and application routes.

Practice task

Compare a proposed repair of an existing rural rental property with a family's purchase of a single-family home. Explain why they should not be routed to the same program by default.

13Multifamily maps and case filesProgram-specific geography, income sources, and scenario practice

Today's mission

  • Find the appropriate Multifamily Housing geographic eligibility information and distinguish it from Single-Family map tools.
  • Practice tracing a multifamily program from its overview to its current handbook, regulation, and funding notice.
  • Review a fictional project and list missing applicant, property, financial, and documentation details.

Practice task

Prepare a one-page case note: program considered, source links, date checked, what appears to fit, what remains unknown, and who should confirm the unresolved points.

14Final review and mock examExplain, compare, calculate, navigate, and document

Today's mission

  • Review all nine listed housing programs and describe each in one or two sentences.
  • Complete the quiz below without notes, then review every explanation.
  • Demonstrate that you can find a current income limit, check the correct eligibility map, and locate official guidance.
  • Identify any formal training, examination, intermediary, or authorization requirements that remain before you can work as a packager.

Ready-to-advance checklist

  • Can I tell Direct from Guaranteed?
  • Can I find, date, and cite the correct income limit?
  • Can I explain what an eligibility map does and does not establish?
  • Can I distinguish home purchase, home repair, multifamily financing, rental assistance, and preservation?
  • Can I verify the 30-day rule from the applicable current source?
