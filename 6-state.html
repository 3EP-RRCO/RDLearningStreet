🎯 How to use this course

For every topic, learn five things: what the program does, who it may serve, how the process works, where the current official rule lives, and how to explain it aloud. You do not need to memorize every number. You do need to know how to verify it and explain what it means.

Important: This is an independent study aid, not an official USDA publication or certification. Requirements, income limits, maps, contacts, funding, and handbook guidance can change. Current official USDA sources control if they differ from this page.

🔎 Four checks you should be able to explain

1. Property eligibility

Use the official tool for the specific program and check the exact address. A rural-looking setting is not proof of eligibility.

2. Household eligibility

Confirm household size, the program’s income definition, allowable adjustments, and the current limit for the location. Different programs can use different income tests.

3. Program fit

Separate Direct from Guaranteed loans, purchase loans from repair assistance, and individual housing from multifamily or community programs.

4. Source and date

Find the current handbook, regulation, official program page, or notice. Record when you checked it and what question the source answers.

📚 Handbook classroom

Open the current consolidated handbook from USDA’s official library. Use its table of contents and revision notices because chapter numbering and policy can change.

HB-1-3550 • Direct Single-Family Housing Loans and Grants

What it covers: Section 502 Direct and related single-family housing guidance.

  • Program framework: purpose, roles, application path, and program structure.
  • Application processing: intake, required documents, completeness review, processing steps, and applicant communication.
  • Borrower eligibility: household, income, assets, credit, repayment capacity, and other requirements.
  • Income calculations: distinguish income used for program eligibility from income used to evaluate repayment. Study definitions, exclusions, adjustments, and worksheets in the current edition.
  • Property requirements: location, property type and use, condition, occupancy, valuation, and applicable standards.
  • Underwriting and loan terms: repayment ability, loan terms, loan limits, payment assistance when available, and approval authority.
  • Closing and servicing: distinguish origination from post-closing account servicing.

Practice: Explain the path from first inquiry to complete application. Name the handbook section you would consult for each decision.

HB-1-3555 • Single-Family Housing Guaranteed Loan Program

What it covers: how a participating lender makes a qualifying loan and USDA provides a guarantee under program rules.

  • Program purpose, eligible loan purposes, and roles of USDA and participating lenders.
  • Applicant eligibility, income definitions, documentation, and current calculation guidance.
  • Creditworthiness, liabilities, repayment ability, and underwriting analysis.
  • Property eligibility, appraisal, condition, occupancy, and location requirements.
  • Loan structure, fees, and applicable limits under current rules.
  • Lender responsibilities and the difference between USDA requirements and lender overlays.
  • Quality control, closing, and requirements before a guarantee is issued.

Practice: Explain Direct versus Guaranteed in 30 seconds without implying that one is automatically better for every applicant.

HB-1-3560 • Multifamily Housing Loan Origination

What it covers: origination guidance for eligible multifamily housing projects.

  • Eligible applicants and project types.
  • Project eligibility, site and development considerations, and application materials.
  • Financial feasibility, underwriting, project costs, and operating assumptions.
  • Affordability, tenant eligibility, rental assistance interactions where applicable, and compliance.
  • Review, approval, closing, and transition to asset management or servicing.

Practice: Compare a single-family borrower file with a multifamily project file. What is being evaluated in each?

Other multifamily references and program families

Review the current HB-1-3565 for the Guaranteed Rural Rental Housing program and HB-2-3560 / HB-3-3560 for applicable multifamily asset management and servicing guidance. Confirm current scope and revisions in USDA’s library.

  • Section 504 Home Repair: repair loans and grants, eligible applicants, repair purposes, and current limitations.
  • Mutual Self-Help Housing: technical assistance for eligible organizations supporting supervised self-help homebuilding.
  • Rental Assistance: its role in eligible multifamily housing, tenant affordability, and applicable rules.
  • Housing Preservation Grants: eligible recipients, activities, and current program guidance.
  • Housing Preservation and Revitalization: eligible applicants, program purpose, current availability, and notices.
  • Community Facilities, Rural Utilities, and Rural Business-Cooperative Service: distinct program families with their own handbooks, regulations, and application processes.

Do not assume Section 502 handbooks govern these other programs.

🧭 State-by-state practice worksheet

  1. Open the official state page and state office contact directory.
  2. Choose a sample town and an exact property address.
  3. Use the official tool for the program you are studying and record the result and date.
  4. Look up current household income limits; record household size, program, and income definition.
  5. Identify the local office or approved-lender path for that program.
  6. Find the handbook or official guidance that answers your question.
  7. Write a three-sentence explanation: what you checked, what the result means, and what still needs review.
State comparison rule: Many core housing rules are national. State pages help locate offices, state notices, and local information; they do not automatically mean each state has different eligibility standards.

🗣️ Speak Rural Development confidently

Answer aloud before opening the model response. Aim for clear, careful explanations, not promises of approval.

“What is the difference between Section 502 Direct and Guaranteed?”

Model answer: “Both are USDA single-family home loan programs, but they work differently. Under Section 502 Direct, USDA makes the loan to an eligible borrower. Under the Guaranteed program, a participating lender makes the loan and USDA provides a guarantee under program rules. Eligibility and application pathways differ, so I would check the current handbook and the household’s circumstances before suggesting a route.”

“If a home is in a rural town, does it qualify?”

Model answer: “Not automatically. I would check the exact property address in the official eligibility tool for the specific program. Location is only one part of the review; the applicant and property must also meet the applicable requirements.”

“Where do you find the income limit?”

Model answer: “I would use USDA’s current eligibility resources for the specific program and location, confirm household size, and check how that program calculates income. I would not reuse an old limit or assume every USDA program uses the same income test.”

“Can you tell me whether this applicant will be approved?”

Model answer: “I can help identify the requirements and information to gather, but approval depends on a complete review by USDA or the participating lender. I would not promise approval based on a quick conversation.”

“How do you know your information is current?”

Model answer: “I check the current consolidated handbook, related procedure notices, the official program page, and relevant state information. I note the date and verify that no newer notice changes the guidance.”
